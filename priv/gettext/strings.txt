Riichi
The classic riichi ruleset, now with an assortment of mods to pick and choose at your liking.
Sanma
Three-player Riichi.
Space Mahjong
Riichi, but sequences can wrap (891, 912), and you can make sequences from winds and dragons. In addition, you can chii from any direction, and form open kokushi (3 han).
Cosmic Riichi
A Space Mahjong variant with mixed triplets, more yaku, and more calls.
Galaxy Mahjong
Riichi, but one of each tile is replaced with a blue galaxy tile that acts as a wildcard of its number. Galaxy winds are wind wildcards, and galaxy dragons are dragon wildcards.
Kansai Sanma
Sanma, but you draw until the last visible dora indicator. In addition, all fives are akadora, fu is fixed at 30, there is no tsumo loss, and scores are rounded to the nearest 1000. Flowers act as nukidora in place of north winds, which are now yakuhai. Exhaustive draws in south round always result in a repeat regardless of who's tenpai.
Zan Sanma
Popular Kansai Sanma variant with a focus on shuugi. Adds shiro pocchi, as well as some yakuman hands: suurenkou, daisharin, nagashi, renhou, manzu honitsu.
Chinitsu
Two-player variant where the only tiles are bamboo tiles. Try not to chombo!
Minefield
Two-player variant where you start with 34 tiles to make a mangan+ hand, and your remaining tiles are your discards.
Speed Tonpuu
Parlor-style riichi with a focus on shuugi payments. Includes blue sevens and a gold five.
Super Bingo (beta)
Sanma but you replace the 19m with four 7p and four 7s, with big shuugi payments.
Sakicards v1.3
Riichi, but everyone gets a different Saki power, which changes the game quite a bit. Some give you bonus han every time you use your power. Some let you recover dead discards. Some let you swap tiles around the entire board, including the dora indicator.
Arushiiaru
Predecessor of Riichi mahjong, which is basically the same thing except scores are 4 times smaller and max out at 2000 (Mangan).
Hochi Rules (beta)
The oldest written ruleset for Riichi, featuring 300 point riichi bets and no fu rounding.
Hong Kong
Hong Kong Old Style mahjong. Three point minimum, everyone pays for a win, and win instantly if you have seven flowers.
Sichuan Bloody
Sichuan Bloody mahjong. Trade tiles, void a suit, and play until three players win (bloody end rules).
MCR
Mahjong Competition Rules. Has a scoring system of a different kind of complexity than Riichi.
Taiwanese
16-tile mahjong with riichi mechanics.
Bloody 30-Faan Jokers
Bloody end rules mahjong, with Vietnamese jokers, and somehow more yaku than MCR.
American (NMJL)
American Mah-Jongg. Assemble hands with jokers, and declare other players' hands dead. Rules are slightly different from MJME 2024 (see rules page).
Vietnamese
Mahjong with eight differently powerful joker tiles.
Malaysian
Three-player mahjong with 16 flowers, a unique joker tile, and instant payouts.
Singaporean
Mahjong with various instant payouts and various unique ways to get penalized by pao.
Chinese Classical
Mahjong but every pung and kong gives you points, and every hand pattern doubles your points.
Fuzhou
16-tile mahjong with a version of dora that doesn't give you han, but becomes a unique winning condition by itself.
Tianjin
Mahjong except the dora indicator actually indicates joker tiles.
Ningbo
Includes Tianjin mahjong joker tiles, but adds more yaku and played with a 4-tai minimum.
Hefei
Mahjong with no honor tiles, but you must have at least eight tiles of a single suit to win.
Zung Jung
Mahjong with an additive (rather than multiplicative) scoring system.
Custom
Create and play your own custom ruleset.

If the AI is stuck or missing, clicking the center compass will try to unstuck them.
Clicking on the center compass will briefly have it show score differences relative to each opponent.
After someone calls a win, hover over the winning hand to see how it breaks into sets. (On mobile: tap the winning hand.)
Public rooms show up in the lobby. The lobby screen only exists if there is at least one public room, otherwise it just drops you directly in a new room.
To join a private room, select the ruleset you're playing, and then press "Join private room" in the main menu and enter the room code of the room you want to join. Then hit Play.
If you scroll down now, or during a game, you can see the JSON representation of the ruleset you're playing. Which you can modify and play in the Custom ruleset.
You can double click or right click to discard your drawn tile or skip a call. (On mobile: double tap or long tap.)
A game's log ID can also be found via the Copy Log button on the upper right.
After a win, you can hover over (or tap) players and arrows in the point exchange screen to view a detailed summary of that transaction.
Click/tap the rules tabs on the left to view the current rules in play.
When viewing the rules, you can hover/tap any tile to enlarge it.
You may apply any custom modifications to the ruleset by typing them in the Config tab of Game Settings. See the documentation link in the Custom gamemode for more details.
The AI for most variants including Riichi use the standard AI, which simply discards towards the closest win condition.
Some variants use a different AI than Riichi's. These variants are Minefield, American, Chinitsu Challenge, and Fuzhou.
Choosing a ruleset (in the Room Settings menu) simply applies a preset set of mods.
Riichi Advanced attempts to be mobile-friendly and tries to support all major browsers. This isn't easy, so if you find something amiss, join the Discord!
Riichi Advanced is open-source, and is currently hosted on GitHub.Clear

By default, American mahjong uses the following 152 tiles:
- Four each of the 1 to 9 of circles %{pinzu}
- Four each of the 1 to 9 of bamboo %{souzu}
- Four each of the 1 to 9 of characters %{manzu}
- Four each of the four winds (East, South, West, North) %{winds}
- Four each of the three dragons (White, Green, Red) %{dragons}
- Four flowers %{flowers}
- Four seasons %{seasons}
- Eight jokers %{jokers}
At the start of the round, each player chooses to pass 3 tiles to the player to the right. This is one charleston pass. A total of 3 charleston passes are done (right, across, left) and if all players agree, 3 more passes are done (left, across, right). Regardless of the decision, there is a final round where each player decides to pass 0 to 3 tiles across, and both player passes the lower amount of the two decisions.
Jokers may not be passed during the Charleston.
(Blind Pass) On the third (and sixth) charleston pass, each player may choose to 'blind pass' up to three tiles, which means instead of passing a tile from hand, pass 1 to 3 of the tiles that are being passed to you this round. In Riichi Advanced, this is done by selecting the upside-down tiles that appear in your hand.
To win, one must exactly match one of the hands on the preselected rules card. The 'nearest hands' button at the bottom can show all nearest hands, which is the easiest way to achieve a win as a beginner. You can also scroll down to see the ruleset's win definitions. The encoding for these definitions is described in the \\\"American hand match specifications\\\" section of 'documentation/documentation.json' on the Riichi Advanced GitHub page.
When it comes to forming a winning hand, jokers cannot be used to fill single tiles or pairs. A hand like '1 22 333' can use up to three jokers for the 333, but no jokers can be used for the 1 or 22. There are no sequences in American Mahjong, only singles, pairs, triplets, quads, and so on.
Note that some hands can only be won while concealed (no calls). These are marked with 'c' to the right of the Nearest Hands hands. 'x' on the other hand means 'exposed' and you can win those hands whether your hand has calls or not.
You cannot win on a discarded joker.
Only one hand can be scored for each win. Typically all hands are disjoint (you can't have more than one) but in the event that the winning hand is ambiguous, Riichi Advanced picks the highest-scoring one.
If you win by discard, everyone pays you the hand value but the discarder's payment is doubled. If you win by self-draw, all players pay double the hand value.
There are by default 8 jokers %{joker} in the wall. Any amount of jokers can be used to call tiles. By default, calls that don't use any non-joker tiles are automatically skipped, but you can deactivate this behavior by toggling off the JC button on the bottom-left (you only need to do this once).
Joker tiles cannot be called (to form a meld, or to win).
You may call Pung/Kong/Quint if a just-discarded tile (not a joker!) matches 2/3/4 of the same tile in hand. You may use any amount of joker tiles for this. Pairs cannot be called. After calling, the involved tiles are shown to all players and cannot be modified. Note that there are no 'replacement tiles' in American mahjong, so in every case you will have to immediately discard from hand after calling. There is no way to 'add' to an existing called meld.
(Joker Swapping) During any player's turn, they can replace a tile in hand if it matches an existing meld on the table (not just their own melds!) that contains a joker. This action swaps the tile in hand with the meld's joker.
If someone's exposed melds indicate that they have no possible winning hand, you can press the skull button for that player to declare their hand dead. If the declaration is correct, then the target player can no longer draw or discard, and must sit out of the rest of the round. If the declaration is incorrect, you suffer that penalty instead.

Malaysian uses the following 84 tiles:
- Four each of the 1 to 9 of Dots %{pinzu}
- Four each of the four Winds (East, South, West, North) %{winds}
- Four each of the three Dragons (White, Green, Red) %{dragons}
- Four Face tiles %{faces}
- Four Animal tiles %{animals}
- Four Flower tiles, numbered from 1 to 4 %{flowers}
- Four Season tiles, numbered from 1 to 4 %{seasons}
- Four Fei Jokers %{feis}
The Face, Animal, Flower, and Season tiles %{flowers} are all considered flower tiles. Their scoring is detailed in the Flowers tab. You also receive a bonus if you declare a set of 4 flowers, see the Rules tab for more details.
The Fei Joker %{fei} is a wildcard that can stand for any tile. They may also be declared as flowers for 1 Fei each by pressing the Reveal Fei button.
You can use up to two Fei Jokers in a called Pong, and one Fei joker in a called Chow. Fei jokers cannot be used in a Gong.
At the end of each round, each player counts the number of Fei jokers in each hand, and recieves a side payment of 50 from each player for each Fei joker they have.
You may win with one of the following:
- Four sets (sequences or triplets) and a pair.
Before each round, each player reveals all their flowers, replaces each with a tile from the wall, and repeats this until they have no flower tiles left.
If you reveal a set of four flowers (%{t1}, %{t2}, %{t3}, or %{t4}) at this stage, then you are immediately paid 100 from each player.
During a round, if a player draws a flower they may replace it with a tile from the wall in the same manner. This is done automatically, but you can turn off this behavior by pressing the F button at the bottom.
If you complete a set of four flowers (%{t1}, %{t2}, %{t3}, or %{t4}) after the initial flower declaration round, then you are immediately paid 50 from each player.
When the player to your left discards a tile you can use to complete a 3 tile sequence, you may call Chow to take that tile instead of drawing.
When any player discards a tile you can use to complete a triplet, you may call Pong to take that tile. Then it becomes your turn to discard.
You may declare Gong to set aside four tiles in three situations:
- (Open) If you have three of a kind, you can claim the fourth if someone discarded it. This awards you an instant payment of 100 from the discarder.
- (Added) If you previously called Pong, you can add the fourth tile to it but only if you draw the tile yourself. This awards you an instant payment of 50 from each player.
- (Concealed) If you have four of a kind, you can declare them as a Concealed Gong. This awards you an instant payment of 100 from each player.
In each case, you must then draw a replacement tile from the back of the wall.
Note that Fei jokers cannot be used in Gongs.
